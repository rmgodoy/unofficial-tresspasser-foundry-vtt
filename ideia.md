I have an idea to run the Haven framework as if the players were the court or council of a city. The city would start in a state of crisis for some reason, and the players' objective would be to make the city grow and prosper.

The city council would meet weekly to make decisions such as what to explore, where to spend money, and how to allocate resources. These decisions would organically generate new adventures.

For example, the city might want to clear out monsters from an abandoned mine to extract resources, improve local equipment manufacturing, and boost trade with other cities. This would prompt a call from the council, creating a new quest.

Another example could be sending explorers to discover new dungeons and then dispatching adventurers to retrieve specific items. There might also be an interest in opening a new road between two cities to establish a trade route, requiring adventurers to safely escort a caravan of workers to clear the path.

The core concept is that while the players build and manage the city on a macro level, they are simultaneously creating quests for their adventurers on a micro level. Tasks and events in the world should progress on their own even when not directly assigned to player characters, ensuring the world feels alive and dynamic.

Players will be allowed to have more than one character. They can assign background quests to their secondary characters and roll on tables to determine their progress and well-being. If their main character dies, they can seamlessly take over one of these secondary characters.
